## Summary

The United States recorded 1,803 data compromises in the first half of 2026, impacting an estimated 471.2 million individuals. The H1 2026 victim total already exceeds the entirety of 2025’s full-year figure of 297.5 million in just six (6) months.

The core driver is a return of mega-breaches after a one-year pause: a single event involving the Canvas education platform (Instructure Holdings) generated an estimated 275 million victim notices, accounting for 58 percent of H1 2026’s total victim count.

At the current pace, 2026 is on track to set a new annual record for the number of data compromises (annualized: ~3,600) continuing a three-year streak of 3,000+ annual compromises.

- **+ 1,803 Total Data Compromises – H1 2026**
- **+ 471,206,085 Victim Notices – H1 2026**
- **+ 1,394 Data Breaches (vs. Exposures, Leaks and Unknown)**  
  Makes up for 77% of total events in H1 2026.
- **+ 76 PERCENT (76%) Breach Notice Lacking Attack Vector Detail**  
  Up from 70 percent (70%) in full-year 2025.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many data breaches happened in the first half of 2026?**  
The ITRC recorded 1,803 total data compromises in H1 2026 (January–June), including 1,394 confirmed data breaches, four (4) data exposures, one (1) data leak, two (2) Previously Compromised Data (PCD) releases and 402 events with unclassified compromise types.

**How many people were affected by data breaches in H1 2026?**  
At least 471.2 million victim notices were issued in H1 2026. This figure already exceeds the entire 2025 full-year total of 297.5 million. The single largest event – the breach of Instructure Holdings’ Canvas platform – accounts for an estimated 275 million of those notices. The confirmed number of U.S.-based victims from Canvas is still pending.

**Is 2026 on track for a record year of data breaches?**  
Yes. If breach notices continue at a similar rate in the final six months of the year as the first half of 2026, the total number of compromises for 2026 could total or exceed 3,600 events – above 2025’s all-time high of 3,321. Q2 2026 alone recorded 1,029 compromises, the second highest single-quarter total in ITRC tracking history.

## Key Findings

The following trends define the H1 2026 data breach landscape. Each represents a material departure from, or continuation of, patterns identified in the ITRC 2025 Annual Data Breach Report.

### Insider Wrongdoing is Surging
- **+ 21 insider wrongdoing events in H1 2026 – more than seven (7) times the full year 2025 total of three (3).**

Insider wrongdoing emerged as a significant, separately tracked attack vector for the first time in 2026, with 21 confirmed incidents in the first half of the year. This is more than seven times the full-year 2025 total of three (3) events. Insider threats are particularly costly because they bypass perimeter defenses, often involve sensitive high-value data, and can go undetected longer than external attacks. The jump may reflect both a genuine increase in malicious insider activity and improved detection capabilities.

### Mega-Breaches Have Returned
- **+ The top two H1 2026 breaches alone (Canvas: 275 million victims; Under Armour: 72.7 million victims) account for more victim notices than the entire 2025 calendar year.**

In 2025, the ITRC identified a shift away from mega-breaches toward more frequent but smaller targeted attacks – the ‘quality over quantity’ pattern. H1 2026 has decisively reversed that trend. The Canvas breach and the Under Armour compromise together generated 347.7 million victim notices. Five (5) of the top ten (10) H1 2026 breaches involve companies with massive consumer databases (education technology, athletic apparel, music streaming, automotive and entertainment), a new cross-sector pattern not previously seen at this scale.

### The Transparency Crisis is Accelerating
- **+ Only 24 percent (24%) of H1 2026 breach notices contained attack vector details – the lowest rate ever recorded. In 2020, nearly 100 percent (100%) of notices disclosed root-cause information.**

The systematic collapse of breach notice transparency, first documented by the ITRC in 2023, is worsening at an accelerating pace. Of 1,803 H1 2026 events, only 425 (24 percent) disclosed how the breach occurred, while 1,378 (76 percent) withheld this critical information. The Cyberattacks: Not Specified subcategory now absorbs 972 events in H1 2026 alone. This opacity prevents consumers, businesses and policymakers from understanding their true risk exposure or taking meaningful preventive action.

### Zero-Day Attacks are Escalating
- **+ 14 zero-day attacks in H1 2026 – approaching the full-year 2025 total of 17 in just six months.**

Zero-day attacks, exploits targeting previously unknown software vulnerabilities, increased to 14 events in H1 2026, up from seven (7) in H1 2025 and are on pace to double the full-year 2025 count. Driven in part by artificial intelligence tools that find software flaws at a rate humans cannot match, these attacks are among the most difficult to defend because no patch exists at the time of exploit. Their rise has particular supply chain implications, as a single zero-day in widely used software can simultaneously expose thousands of downstream organizations.

### Public Companies: Extreme Concentration of Data Equals High-Value Targets
- **+ Publicly traded companies account for 10 percent (10%) of compromises but 83 percent (83%) of breach notices.**

Publicly traded companies accounted for 185 of 1,803 H1 2026 compromises (10.3 percent), yet generated 392.9 million victim notices – 83.4 percent (83.4%) of the total. This extreme concentration reflects the scale of consumer data held by large public enterprises and their attractiveness as high-value targets. It also has implications for SEC disclosure requirements and investor expectations around cybersecurity risk management.

### The ‘Not Specified’ Problem

Of 1,256 H1 2026 cyberattacks, 972 (77.4 percent) list ‘Not Specified’ as the attack vector. This means that for more than three out of four cyberattack-related breach notices, the method of compromise is simply unknown to the public and to other potential victims. The ‘Not Specified’ count (972) is already at 49.8 percent (49.8%) of the full-year 2025 total (1,948), suggesting that transparency is not improving – it is deteriorating at a structural level.

### What is Driving Insider Wrongdoing?

Research and trade media coverage focus and converge on seven distinct driver categories. The H1 2026 spike in insider wrongdoing events reflects a combination of several factors, but two are the most novel and fastest-growing: layoffs in the tech sector and North Korea placing IT workers in U.S. companies.

1. **Workforce Volatility: Layoffs and Departing Employees**  
   Data theft spikes 720 percent (720%) in the 24 hours before a layoff announcement, with suspicious exfiltration patterns beginning as early as six months prior. Organizations undergoing restructuring experience 3.2 times more insider incidents than stable organizations. The ~245,000 tech layoffs announced in 2025 alone created an unusually large pool of disgruntled or departing employees with active system access – a classic high-risk window that many organizations still fail to close quickly enough through deprovisioning.

2. **Nation-State Recruitment: The North Korea IT Worker Problem**  
   This is arguably the most significant structural driver of malicious insider attacks. North Korean operatives have systematically infiltrated U.S. employers as remote IT workers using stolen identities, deepfake video during interviews and AI-generated resumes at scale. Okta Threat Intelligence documented more than 6,500 interviews across 500 companies by 130+ DPRK-linked actors. Amazon alone blocked 1,800 suspected North Korean applications between April 2024 and December 2025. Once embedded, these workers steal code, harvest credentials and – increasingly – engage in extortion: threatening to leak proprietary data unless paid. DOJ indictments and sentencings in 2025–2026 confirmed the scheme’s reach. The FBI’s IC3 issued a formal public service announcement in July 2025 specifically on this threat.

### Supply Chain Attack: The Multiplier Effect

Supply chain attacks generated 280.6 million victim notices in H1 2026 from just 38 initial breach events affecting 206 entities. This ‘multiplier effect’, where a single breach cascades into dozens of affected organizations, makes supply chain exposure the most consequential attack vector for aggregate victim impact. Supply chain cyberattacks alone accounted for 199 of 206 affected entities and 280.6 million of 280.6 million combined victim notices.

### Previously Compromised Data (PCD): A Growing Threat

H1 2026 recorded two PCD events – data releases consisting primarily of previously stolen records that have been repackaged for new attacks. The second event, discovered June 12, 2026, involved an unsecured Elasticsearch cluster exposing approximately 24 billion records. Since the data consists mostly of material from prior breaches, the ITRC classified it as PCD rather than a new breach, and victim notice counts are not yet confirmed.[^2] PCD events are particularly dangerous because they enable AI-assisted credential stuffing and account takeover at scale, using data that victims and organizations may have assumed was no longer actionable.

## Advice For Customers

With 471 million victim notices in just six months, in a country of approximately 340 million people, the statistical likelihood that your personal information has been exposed in 2026 is extremely high. Most people cannot rely on organizations to protect their data; the following steps are the most effective actions individuals can take to limit their exposure and reduce the impact of a breach.

### Before You Receive a Breach Notice: ACTIVE PROTECTION

**What is the most effective step a consumer can take to prevent identity theft, fraud and scams?**  
Freezing your credit files at all four national credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion and Innovis), and ChexSystems (new bank accounts), is the single most effective fraud prevention tool available to consumers. A credit freeze prevents new accounts from being opened in your name, even if criminals have your Social Security Number.

Freezing your credit and personal information is free, can be done online and does not affect your credit score. For assistance, visit ITRC’s freeze information website **FrozenPII.com** or contact an ITRC expert advisor by chat, phone or text.

Other important steps to take include:
- **Switch to Passkeys:** Replace passwords with passkeys wherever possible. Passkeys, unlike passwords, cannot be stolen in a database breach because the organization never stores your secret code.
- **Do Not Repeat Passwords:** For accounts where a passkey is not offered, use a password manager to generate and store unique, complex passwords for every account. Credential stuffing attacks, which exploit reused passwords, accounted for nine of the H1 2026 events affecting tens of thousands of victims.
- **Enable MFA:** If passkeys are not offered, enable multi-factor authentication on all sensitive accounts, especially email, banking and retirement accounts. Use an authenticator app rather than SMS codes, which can be intercepted in a SIM-swapping attack.

### After Receiving a Breach Notice: REACTIVE ACTION

**What should you do immediately after receiving a data breach notice?**
- **Verify the Notice First:** Go to the organization’s official website – not links in the notice email – to confirm it is legitimate before taking any action.
- **Change Passwords Immediately:** Change the password on the affected account immediately. If you use the same password elsewhere, change every account that shares it. Prioritize email and banking.
- **Enroll in Offered Monitoring:** Sign up for any free credit monitoring or identity protection services offered in the notice.
- **Expect Follow-On Attacks:** Stay alert to unexpected password reset emails, new account alerts or changes to financial statements.
- **Heighten Phishing Vigilance:** The ITRC’s 2025 Annual Data Breach Report found that 53.7 percent (53.7%) of breach victims experienced increased phishing attempts after a breach. Treat any unsolicited contact from the breached organization as suspicious until you verify all communications with them.

### Special Considerations from H1 2026 Trends

- **CANVAS Users:** If you use Canvas for education or work, assume your information has been exposed. Canvas reported 275 million affected accounts. Change your Canvas password to a passkey or a strong, unique password immediately and watch for phishing attempts impersonating educational institutions.
- **UNDER ARMOUR/FITNESS APP Users:** If you have an Under Armour (MapMyRun, MapMyFitness, MyFitnessPal) account, change your password and check for unauthorized use.
- **SOUNDCLOUD Users:** If you use SoundCloud, update your credentials and ensure you do not reuse that password on other platforms, apps or accounts.

## Advice For Businesses

H1 2026’s data reveals that businesses of all sizes face a threat landscape that has grown more complex, more frequent and more costly. The following recommendations are grounded in the specific patterns observed in H1 2026 data and are appropriate for organizations across all sectors.

### Addressing the INSIDER THREAT SURGE

**How should businesses respond to the rise in insider wrongdoing?**  
Implement least-privilege access controls, ensuring employees can only access data required for their specific role. Pair this with behavior analytics and audit logging to detect anomalous data access patterns early. Regular access reviews and immediate deprovisioning upon employee termination or role change are essential.

- **Conduct an Access Audit Now:** H1 2026’s 21 insider wrongdoing events (seven (7) times the full-year 2025 total) indicate immediate attention is required. Conduct a full access rights review and remove all unnecessary system privileges.
- **Deploy Behavior Analytics:** Implement user and entity behavior analytics to identify unusual data access, exfiltration or download patterns before a breach occurs.
- **Automate Deprovisioning:** Immediately revoke all system access upon employee termination, role change or resignation. Insider data breaches often occur in this transition window.

### SUPPLY CHAIN RISK MANAGEMENT

- **Vet Your Vendors Continuously:** Require all direct vendors and third-party service providers to demonstrate real-time security monitoring capabilities. H1 2026’s 206 supply chain-affected entities (vs. 38 initial attacks) demonstrate that indirect exposure through vendors is often larger than direct exposure.
- **Monitor Your Supply Chain in Real Time:** Subscribe to the ITRC’s Breach Alert for Business service to receive near real-time notifications when any direct or indirect vendor appears in the ITRC breach database.
- **Update Vendor Contracts:** Update vendor contracts to include mandatory disclosure timelines, minimum security standards and breach notification requirements consistent with the shortest applicable state law (as little as 15 days in some jurisdictions).

### RANSOMWARE & ZERO-DAY PREPAREDNESS

- **Maintain a Dual Defense Posture:** Ransomware’s reversal in H1 2026 (76 events vs. 73 in H1 2025) and zero-day attacks’ sharp increase (14 events, on pace to double 2025’s total) require a dual posture: maintain robust backup and recovery capabilities while prioritizing rapid patching and vulnerability management.
- **Automate Patch Management:** Implement automated patch management that does not rely on manual intervention. Zero-day attacks, by definition, cannot be patched preemptively, but automated runtime rules-based protections block known and zero-day attacks.
- **Update Incident Response Plans:** Run tabletop exercises that simulate both ransomware and data-theft-only extortion scenarios. The shift away from encryption means that backup recovery alone is no longer an adequate response plan.

### CYBERSECURITY ARCHITECTURE & ACCESS CONTROLS

- **Adopt Zero Trust Architecture:** Implement a Zero Trust security model – assume no user, device or network segment is trusted by default. Verify continuously, limit lateral movement and require strong authentication for every access request.
- **Mandate Passkeys for Privileged Access:** Require passkeys or hardware security keys for all privileged and administrative access. Phishing/Smishing/BEC remains the #1 named attack vector (157 events in H1 2026); passkeys are immune to credential phishing.
- **Segment Your Networks:** With 14 zero-day attacks in H1 2026, organizations should assume unknown vulnerabilities exist. Network segmentation limits the blast radius of any single exploit.

### Breach Transparency & Notification

**Are businesses required to disclose the cause of the data breach?**  
Requirements vary by state. Currently, only 24 percent of H1 2026 breach notices voluntarily disclose how the breach occurred. While disclosure of root cause is not universally mandated, organizations that provide clear, factual, non-technical explanations of what happened build greater consumer trust, reduce litigation risk through demonstrated good faith and help other organizations defend against similar attacks.

- **Choose Transparency Over Opacity:** Voluntarily disclosing attack vectors in breach notices is both the ethical and increasingly the strategic choice. With 76 percent of H1 2026 notices suppressing this information, organizations that do disclose differentiate themselves as trustworthy stewards.
- **Standardize Your Breach Notices:** Develop breach notification templates that include: what data was exposed, how it happened (in plain language), what the organization is doing to prevent recurrence and step-by-step instructions for affected individuals.

### EMPLOYEE TRAINING & CULTURE

- **Train on Correspondence Handling:** Correspondence (email/letter)-based errors accounted for 69 of 125 H1 2026 system and human error events (55 percent). Regular, mandatory training on secure email handling, data handling procedures and social engineering recognition remains essential.
- **Build an Insider Threat Awareness Culture:** Insider threat awareness programs should normalize reporting of suspicious colleague behavior without creating a culture of fear – the goal is early detection, not surveillance.
- **Conduct Regular Phishing Simulations:** Run phishing simulations regularly and benchmark click rates. With 157 phishing/BEC events in H1 2026, employees are still a critical vulnerability and a critical defense.

## About This Report

This report is based on the Identity Theft Resource Center’s final H1 2026 Data Compromise Statistics (as of July 1, 2026) and the ITRC’s 2025 Annual Data Breach Report (published January 2026). The ITRC has tracked public data breach notifications since 2005, compiling one of the most comprehensive breach databases in the United States.

The ITRC aggregates data events based on the date they are entered into the Breach Alert database rather than the date the underlying compromise occurred. Victim notice counts are the number of notices issued, not necessarily a one-to-one count of unique individuals, as one person may receive multiple notices from multiple events. H1 2026 data remains subject to revision as additional notices are processed and reported.

### Key Definitions

- **Data Breach:** An event where unauthorized individuals access and/or remove personal information from the place where it is stored.
- **Data Exposure:** When personal information is available for access but there is no evidence it has been accessed by unauthorized individuals.
- **Data Leak:** When aggregated publicly available personal data creates risk to individuals when combined.
- **Previously Compromised Data (PCD):** Information stolen in past breaches that is later repackaged, aggregated, or recirculated – often using AI – for new attacks.
- **Supply Chain Attack:** A breach caused by a vendor, supplier, or third-party service provider rather than directly by the affected organization.

### Methodology Notes

For purposes of quarterly and annual reporting, the ITRC aggregates data events based on the date the breach, exposure, or leak was entered into the database rather than the date the event occurred. This avoids the confusion and data conflicts associated with the need to routinely update previous reports and compromise totals. The date of the original compromise, if known, and the date of the event report are noted in the ITRC’s Breach Alert data compromise tracking database.

The number of victims linked to individual compromises are updated as needed and can be accessed in the ITRC’s Breach Alert breach tracking solution. The ITRC reports Third-Party/Supply Chain Attacks as a single attack against the company that lost control of the information. The total number of individuals impacted by third-party incidents is based on notices sent by the multiple organizations impacted by the single data compromise.
