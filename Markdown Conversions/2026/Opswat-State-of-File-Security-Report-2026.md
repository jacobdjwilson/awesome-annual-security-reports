## Executive summary

Files are under sustained attack, and AI is intensifying both the threats coming in and the data leaking out. Security leaders believe their defenses are stronger than the evidence shows. That gap will widen as AI-driven threats accelerate. Something needs to change.

This report presents the data from a recent research initiative exploring the threat and protection dynamics of enterprise files in the age of AI. It examines the effects of the paradigm shift from detection to prevention, where confidence in security posture measurably increases, and assesses the distance between self-assessed maturity for file security and what the evidence actually supports (it’s significant). Finally, it proposes a set of grounded actions for organizations to pursue as they strengthen their file security posture for a rapidly intensifying AI-driven threat landscape.

### KEY TAKEAWAYS

The key takeaways from this research are:

- **AI is accelerating threats against files**  
  Nearly 60% of respondents expect AI to make file security more difficult for their IT and security teams. That concern is already climbing—worry about AI-driven threats to files has risen by almost 80% across the 24-month window examined, with generative AI and large language model threats rising fastest, up 105%.

- **The maturity gap is severe: the data shows a striking contradiction**  
  73.6% of respondents self-assess their file security posture as very or extremely mature. But correlating respondents’ actual answers across the survey against a maturity framework shows that no organization gives evidence of either of the top two levels of maturity, and 85.8% evidence no more than “somewhat mature.” What leaders believe about their posture is unsupported by their own answers.

- **Threat actors are actively targeting files**  
  68.1% of respondents say that cybercriminals are more focused on circumventing their file security protections than 12 months ago.

- **IT and security leaders are conflicted about the way forward**  
  27.2% of respondents strongly agree a paradigm shift away from a detection-first approach is needed, the highest agreement of any file security posture statement tested. Yet many still endorse detection-based approaches. The industry is in transition and knows where it needs to go—but hasn’t gotten there yet.

- **Barriers are human, not technical**  
  The top two reasons why prevention-first solutions aren’t deployed are lack of leadership support and lack of knowledge—both human barriers, not technical or cost ones.

> The severe gap between capability and confidence for file security will continue to widen as AI-driven threats rapidly intensify—unless human barriers to better file security posture are urgently addressed.

## Risks and threats to file security

Are files a security threat for organizations? This section looks at the current risks and threats to file security, including threat actor interest, employee actions, and data breach consequences.

### THREAT ACTORS ARE MORE INTERESTED IN CIRCUMVENTING FILE SECURITY PROTECTIONS

68.1% of the respondents in this research say that the activity and targets of cybercriminals over the past 12 months show growing interest in circumventing their organization’s file security protections. Only 15% of respondents say that cybercriminals are less interested. See Figure 1.

> Cybercriminals are increasingly interested in circumventing file security protections.

#### Figure 1: Interest by cybercriminals in circumventing file security protections over the past 12 months
![Figure 1: Interest by cybercriminals in circumventing file security protections over the past 12 months (Percentage of respondents)]  
*Source: Osterman Research (2026)*

Examples of this growing interest in circumventing file security protections include:

- High levels of risk from files coming into an organization (e.g., files downloaded from unknown sources) combined with low confidence in ability to prevent malware and other file-related threats from compromising files through those same methods.
- Growing levels of concern for file security due to the use of AI by threat actors, such as using AI to generate threats that evade AI-powered security defenses. On average, the level of concern increases by almost 80% for a range of AI-driven threats over the 24-month timeframe we asked about.
- Looking ahead over the next 12 months, 59% of respondents anticipate heightened impact on the time taken to deal with file-related security activities due to the use of AI by threat actors. Most respondents expect file security to become more difficult due to offensive AI.

## From detection-first to prevention-first

Detection has been the dominant approach for neutralizing threats for decades. Does it still work given the modern and emerging threat landscape?

### DETECTION-FIRST POSTURE: CRACKS IN THE FOUNDATION

A detection-first posture to file-related threats is the belief that cybersecurity solutions should be designed to detect the presence of cyber threats in files, e.g., malicious content and damaging components. By implication, measuring the efficacy of different detection-first products is done by comparing the difference in detection rates and mitigation actions. Surprisingly, the majority of respondents in this research agree or strongly agree with two opposing perspectives that they rated independently: that eliminating all file-related threats is merely about spending more to detect all threats and that a paradigm-shift away from a detection-first posture would be required to make significant improvements in file security. In the aggregate, this stance doesn’t make sense.

To resolve the apparent contradiction, the change in the “strongly agree” rating across the statements in Figure 5 below offers the key. The highest rating is for the need of a paradigm shift away from a detection-first posture (27.2%), followed by the realization that an organization can never spend enough under a detection-first posture to eliminate all file-related threats (21.3%). In last place is the idea that a detection-first posture has ongoing validity (18.9%). See Figure 5.

> While a detection-first approach is deeply entrenched, support for a new paradigm is significant.

### EMERGING AI-DRIVEN THREATS ARE CHANGING THE DYNAMICS OF CYBER RISK

The urgent need for the paradigm shift to prevention-first is amplified by what’s happening on the AI threat side. Concern about AI-driven threats to files are growing rapidly—from an average across the six threat types of 27.8% of respondents with high or extreme concern 12 months ago to an average of 49.3% with an anticipation of high or extreme concern in 12 months. That’s an average increase of 78.9% from 12 months ago to anticipated concern in 12 months. See Figure 6.

AI-driven threats to files that evidence the highest increase in concern are:

- **Unmanaged data ingress and egress via generative AI and LLMs (105% increase)**  
  When new AI services ignore data governance policies for data entering and leaving the organization, therefore creating data risk and exposure.
- **AI-generated threats that can evade AI-powered security defenses (87% increase)**  
  When threat actors use AI to create threats with advanced capabilities to bypass the AI-powered security defenses implemented by organizations.
- **Data exfiltration and IP leakage through public LLMs (79% increase)**  
  When employees unintentionally or unwittingly share corporate data with a public LLM to accomplish a task (e.g., summarize, find related data, fact check), failing to recognize the sensitive nature of the data they have exposed.

> Average concern about emerging AI-driven threats to files increases by 78.9% over the three time periods we asked about.

### TECHNOLOGIES USED FOR SECURING FILES—CURRENT AND OUTLOOK

### ORGANIZATIONS WITH A PREVENTION-FIRST MINDSET AND APPROACH HAVE HIGHER CONFIDENCE LEVELS

To test the impact of a prevention-first mindset and approach on confidence levels to prevent malware and other file-related threats, we split the respondents into two groups:

- **Group one.** Respondents who give evidence of a prevention-first mindset and approach. Specifically, we looked for respondents that met two tests: they agreed on the need for a paradigm shift to file security, and they are currently using content disarm and reconstruction. This netted 102 respondents (40.2% of respondents in this research).
- **Group two.** All other respondents in the research—152 (59.8%)—who do not meet the above two tests and therefore do not give evidence of a prevention-first mindset and approach.

When the confidence data from Figure 8 above is correlated by whether the organization evidences a prevention-first mindset and approach (group one) or not (group two), average confidence levels at both the very confident and extremely confident levels are higher for group one than group two. See Figure 9 on the next page.

Across the 10 situations where employees send or receive files, the data says:

- In 8 of the 10 situations, organizations with a prevention-first mindset and approach (group one) exhibit higher levels of extreme confidence that they can prevent malware and other file-related threats versus everyone else (group two).
- The variation at the extremely confident level for group one is particularly noticeable for the approaches that cross organizational boundaries, e.g., receiving files from third parties via managed file transfer services (91.6% higher confidence at the extremely confident level).
- In 2 of the 10 situations, the group one organizations have lower levels of confidence at the extremely confident level compared to group two—this is shown as -16.8% for employees accessing files stored in on-premises repositories, and -2.4% for employees downloading files from internal business applications. The negative numbers mean that the organizations in group two said their confidence was higher. Both situations are for internal systems under the direct control of the organization, and hence where other file security approaches are active and the impact of content disarm and reconstruction is less noticeable (by design).
- There is less of a differential between the confidence rates of the two groups for internal systems under the direct control of the organization, likely because other file security approaches are active. The highest percentage variation for internal systems at either the very confident or extremely confident level is 28.8% (negative), while the highest percentage variation for the external systems is 91.6%.

Overall, while a prevention-first mindset and approach does not give evidence of an uplift in confidence across all 10 situations, the dominant pattern by far is that the level of extreme confidence is higher for group one, and that this effect is particularly noticeable for systems that cross organizational boundaries.

> Organizations embracing a prevention-first strategy are more likely to be extremely confident that they can prevent malware and other file-related threats.

### CURRENT TECHNOLOGIES AREN’T ASSURING CONFIDENCE IN PROTECTING FILES FROM EMERGING THREATS

Given the technologies for file security that organizations are currently using, an average of only 60.6% of respondents indicate high confidence in their ability to detect and prevent emerging types of threats to files. Out of all respondents, only 21.6% say they have the highest level of confidence. See Figure 10.

> Only 21.6% of respondents have the highest confidence that they can detect and prevent a range of emerging threats to files.

#### Figure 10: Confidence to detect and prevent emerging threats to files
![Figure 10: Confidence to detect and prevent emerging threats to files (Percentage of respondents and sum of the two confidence statements)]  
*Source: Osterman Research (2026)*  
*\* sum does not match due to rounding of the two underlying numbers*

The confidence portrayed in Figure 10 is perplexing for two reasons:

- Average confidence for protecting files from new and emerging threats (Figure 10) is higher than average confidence for protecting files from long-running threat types such as malware (see Figure 8). In principle, confidence for long-running threat types should be higher, because organizations have more experience in defending against incidents and attacks over multiple waves and changes in their defensive posture.
- The lowest result for the extremely confident rating in Figure 10 is polymorphic threats that can evade signature-based defenses (12.2%). This is the longest-running threat type of the six emerging threats and yet has the lowest level of extreme confidence. In principle, confidence levels to detect and prevent this threat type should set the high mark for the other types.

Whether the confidence levels shown in Figure 10 are based on pre-sales vendor hype or post-sales incident data will be tested over the coming 12–24 months.

### ORGANIZATIONAL PRACTICES FOR FILE SECURITY ARE MUCH LESS MATURE THAN WHAT RESPONDENTS BELIEVE

73.6% of respondents self-assess that their file security posture, after taking into consideration everything they are doing to address the threats against files in their organization, is very or extremely mature. Maturity in this case means an assessment of how well the organization is positioned to respond to threats against files, encompassing their use of technology, their approach to training and readiness of people, and the efficacy of their security processes. See Figure 11.

To test the accuracy of this self-assessment, we correlated the data given by each respondent across the survey to create an evidence-based maturity model, with data analyzed from multiple perspectives. We combined the analysis from each perspective to derive an aggregated maturity rating for each organization. In looking at the variations between claimed and evidenced maturity, the data says:

- Only 2% of organizations evidence the same level of maturity that they claim. A further 3.5% of organizations evidence a higher level of maturity than what they claim. In combination, a mere 5.5% evidence the same or higher level of maturity than what they claim.
- The majority—by a long way—over-assess their maturity compared to the evidence of their actual file security posture (94.5%). Their claim of maturity is undermined by their actual approach.
- Across all respondents, the weight of evidence shows that actual maturity is significantly lower than claimed maturity. Most organizations are only “somewhat mature” (85.8%) given how they actually approach file security. See Figure 11.

> 94.5% of organizations assess their maturity with file security at a higher level than the evidence allows.

## Exploring gaps in file security

What’s driving the gap in file security posture for organizations—and what does the gap cost in terms of data breaches and cybersecurity incidents?

### REASONS WHY ORGANIZATIONS ARE NOT USING FILE SECURITY SOLUTIONS – LACK OF KNOWLEDGE IS KEY

An average of 42.6% of respondents indicated their organization has no current plans to use one or more of the nine file security solutions we queried in the survey. We asked respondents to rate the importance of seven different reasons why they were not currently using each of these solutions.

Figure 13 lists the average ranking of the seven reasons across the nine file security solution types, with the most important reason at the top of the list being that senior leadership doesn’t support the use of the solution. Second place is where respondents don’t know enough about how the solution could help their organization. The least important reason is that the solution doesn’t fit with their organization’s current cybersecurity strategy.

> New file security solutions aren’t used due to lack of leadership support and poor knowledge – human factors, not technical ones.

#### Figure 13: Reasons why file security solutions are not used–aggregated order

Leadership support (first place) and hence budget (third place) and the availability of people (fourth place) cannot go where knowledge has not already been.

### STRENGTHEN SECURITY POSTURE AGAINST EMERGING THREAT TYPES

Respondents indicate a doubling of the priority at their organization for strengthening their security posture to address emerging threat types, with new AI-driven threats high on the list. The emerging threat types shown in Figure 20 will increasingly affect all organizations with greater intensity, driving costly data breaches and cybersecurity incidents that undermine customer trust dynamics and damage organizational data.

For the organizations in this research, the data (see Figure 20) says the highest increase in priority is to address:

- **AI-generated threats that can evade AI-powered security defenses** (111% growth in priority from 12 months ago to anticipated priority in 12 months).
- **Undisclosed AI in work product submitted by third parties** (110%). Doing so could have contractual implications.
- **AI-generated files containing falsified information or falsified provenance signals** (108%).

Organizations with sufficiently mature deployments of file security solutions against these specific threats may not need to place as high a priority on addressing them. However, as threat dynamics change over the next 12 months, relative solution efficacy may decline.

> Emerging threat types will increasingly affect all organizations, driving data breaches and cybersecurity incidents.

### DRIVE ELEVATED MATURITY IN APPLYING AND ENFORCING CONSISTENT FILE SECURITY POLICIES ACROSS CHANNELS

Inconsistent file security policies across the channels employees regularly use results in threats against files slipping through unnoticed. The average at the extremely mature level across the nine channels is 26.5%. See Figure 21.

The channel with the highest level of maturity in applying and enforcing consistent file security policies for the organizations in this research is email (covering attachments and links), at only 32.3%. While this is the highest result, it is low for a channel so frequently used by employees for work processes and threat actors for unleashing cyberattacks. You will recall from Figure 2 that employees receiving files via email or links was the second highest risk.

Organizations seeking stronger protections against existing and emerging cyber threats which compromise files will need to:

- Implement new file security solutions where they are missing;
- Drive elevated deployment maturity where they are already deployed; and
- Invest in either a centralized policy hub or a policy orchestration engine that works across the channels used within the organization.

> **Key action point:** Apply and enforce consistent file security policies across all the channels employees use in carrying out their work.

## Conclusion

Files are already under increasing attack and new emerging threat types—particularly those that leverage rapidly-advancing offensive AI capabilities—are amplifying the risk of costly data breaches and cybersecurity incidents. Most organizations are under-prepared for the coming wave of attacks, and far too many lack confidence that their current security posture is up to the task. The data in this research demands an urgent re-evaluation of how files are secured, one that more than merely seeks to detect current and emerging threats better than yesterday. An investigation into the benefits of investing in prevention-first approaches should be high on the priority list for cybersecurity decision-makers.

> The data in this research demands an urgent re-evaluation of how files are secured.
