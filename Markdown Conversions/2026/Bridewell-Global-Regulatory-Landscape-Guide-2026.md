What’s New: Global
Global: New Comprehensive Privacy Regimes, AI regulation and Cross-border Transfer Compliance
Outside the EU/UK and USA, regulation is accelerating, with many jurisdictions moving toward
enforceable privacy, tighter cross-border transfer controls, stronger cyber security oversight, and early
but fast-maturing AI governance.
January 2026 marks the effective date for several major Asia-Pacific measures: Vietnam’s Personal Data
Protection Law takes effect on 1 January 2026, introducing a comprehensive framework with
cross-border transfer conditions and significant penalties; China’s Cybersecurity Law amendments also
take effect on 1 January 2026, reinforcing cybersecurity governance and linking it to AI
security/development; and China’s Measures for Authentication of Personal Information Exported Abroad
begin the same day, adding a certification-based pathway for certain international transfers.
March 2026 brings a further AI milestone, with Vietnam’s AI Law effective 1 March 2026, embedding
fairness, transparency and accountability principles and higher expectations for riskier applications.
Other themes stand out across different jurisdictions:
• Cross-border transfer compliance is becoming more structured, with Chile’s Model Contractual
Clauses (valid until December 2026) and India’s DPDPA Rules phasing in through May 2027
(alongside proposed amendments affecting children’s protections).
• Comprehensive privacy regimes and enforcement readiness are expanding (e.g., Gambia’s DP law
and proposed reforms in Bangladesh and Colombia). A longer-horizon datapoint is Paraguay’s DP act
listed for November 2027, reinforcing continued global convergence.
• Cybersecurity and “trustworthy AI” controls are tightening, including Singapore’s expanded
cybersecurity oversight, Canada’s proposed critical cyber systems framework, and practical AI
security/assessment guidance from South Korea and Australia.
