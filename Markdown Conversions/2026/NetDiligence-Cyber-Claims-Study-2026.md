## Introduction

Welcome to the sixteenth annual NetDiligence® Cyber Claims Study. This report is based on the summary statistical analysis of over 10,300 cyber claims for incidents that occurred during the five-year period 2021–2025. By comparison, the first Cyber Claims Study, published in 2010, analyzed fewer than 100 cyber insurance claims.

### By the Numbers
- 10,309 claims analyzed, arising from incidents occurring 2021–2025
- 4,825 new and updated claims collected in 2025, from incidents occurring 2023–2025
- 1,780 claims analyzed arising from incidents occurring in 2025
- 97% of claims ($2.5B in total) from small to medium enterprises (SMEs) with less than $2 billion in annual revenue
- 3% of claims ($3.2B in total) from large companies with more than $2 billion in annual revenue
- 2,639 claims due to ransomware, 57% of which occurred between 2023 and 2025
- 2,170 claims at SMEs due to business email compromise, 69% of which occurred between 2023 and 2025

## Key Findings

- We see enormous variances in the magnitude of loss data. The smallest claims were less than $1,000; the largest were over $500M. The numbers of records exposed ranged from 1 to over 140M.
- There were dramatic differences between the numbers for SMEs and for large companies—multiples of 10x, 1000x, or more. The biggest large company in the dataset (over $290B in annual revenue) was approximately 29 million times larger than the smallest organization (less than $10K in annual revenue). The average large company ($10.1B in annual revenue) was almost 100 times larger than the average SME ($105M).
- Even though large companies represented only 3% of claims (N=324), these claims accounted for 56% of the total incident cost analyzed in the report ($3.2B/$5.7B).
- The dataset contains 12 claims >$100M, 68 claims $10M–$99M, and 403 claims $1M–$10M. Of the 12 claims >$100M, two occurred at organizations with <$700M in annual revenue.
- In SMEs, there were 381 claims ≥$1M (4%). In large companies, there were 92 claims ≥$1M (28%).
- Ransomware and business email compromise were the two leading causes of loss. At SMEs, they accounted for 51% of claims ≥$1K in the five-year period 2021–2025, and nearly 64% in 2025.
- Ransoms rose to new and unprecedented levels, with initial demands as high as $500M and ransoms paid as high as $90M. There were 59 ransoms paid ≥$10M.

In short, the cost of cyber incidents continues to rise.

### TERMS
- **Breach Coach**: A qualified data security and privacy attorney who provides legal guidance for cyber response.
- **Incident Cost**: Because the proportion of "recordless" events is so large, we replaced the term "breach" with "incident." The term incident cost in this report means the aggregate total of all types of costs/expenses associated with the incident.
- **Crisis Services Costs**: Costs associated with responding to the breach event. These costs include, but are not limited to, breach coach counsel, forensics, notification, credit/ID monitoring, and public relations.
- **Legal Costs**: Legal and regulatory expenses incurred due to the event. These costs include, but are not limited to, lawsuit defense, lawsuit settlement, regulatory action defense, and regulatory fines.
- **Self-Insured Retention (SIR)**: The dollar amount that the insured organization had to pay before the insurer paid anything on the claim. In this study, the SIR is included in incident cost.
- **Small to Medium Enterprise (SME)**: Categorized in this study as organizations with less than $2 billion in annual revenue.
- **Large Company**: Categorized in this study as organizations with $2 billion or more in annual revenue.

## An Overview of the Data

The claims analyzed in this study come from organizations of all sizes, the smallest with less than $10K in annual revenue and the largest with over $290B. As the dataset is overwhelmingly weighted with claims from smaller companies, this may dilute the findings for large companies. Likewise, large companies can function as outliers, skewing the findings for small organizations. Therefore, the dataset has been divided into two categories based on the size of the insured entity. Organizations with less than $2B in annual revenue are defined as small to medium enterprises (SMEs), while those with $2B or greater in annual revenue are defined as large companies.

The majority of study participants (71%) provided estimates of the annual revenue of the insured entities. Analysis of this data provides the following company demographics:
- **SMEs**: annual revenue ranged from less than $10K to $1.9B. The average was $105M. SMEs accounted for 97% of claims but only 44% of total incident cost.
- **Large companies**: annual revenue ranged from $2B to more than $290B. The average was $10.1B. Large companies accounted for only 3% of claims but 56% of total incident cost.

## Incident Cost and Payout

Study participants were asked to provide information about the amount of money paid on a claim and to give an estimate of the total cost of the incident, including self-insured retention (SIR) and other costs that may have been excluded due to the terms of the policy.

There were 381 SME claims over $1M, and another 345 claims $500K–$1M. The largest SME claims occurred in 2022 (>$100M). These incidents happened in the manufacturing and healthcare sectors. Both involved ransomware with very large ransoms and extremely large business interruption losses (>$90M). Neither company was extremely large—annual revenue for each was <$700M.

The largest incident at a large company occurred in 2021 (>$500M). Between 2021 and 2025, there were 15 claims at large companies with over $50M in total incident cost, and another 27 claims with $10M-$50M in total incident cost.

Payouts for all organization sizes covered 39% of the total incident cost. For SMEs, the five-year payout was 67% of the total incident cost. At large companies, this number was 20%.

For SMEs, these proportions are similar to last year’s report (67% vs 64%). For large companies, they also remain steady (20% vs 22%).

Figures 9 and 10 provide year-by-year averages of payout versus total incident cost, plus the five-year averages of payout amount and total incident cost for both SMEs and large companies.

The five-year numbers for SMEs are similar to those reported last year. Average total incident cost and payouts for 2021-2025 were $266K and $179K, versus $264K and $183K for 2020-2024.

At large companies, the numbers also remained steady. Average total incident cost and payouts for 2021-2025 were $11.2M and $2.2M, compared to $10.3M and $2.8M for the period 2020-2024.

## Business Interruption (BI)

### SMEs
BI costs were reported for 313 incidents. Since 2021, the average BI cost and corresponding average incident cost have remained high. The spike in 2022 was caused by two very large outlier incidents, each in excess of $100M.

Additional analysis shows that the five-year average incident cost of a claim with BI was over 500% greater than a claim without BI. In 2025, the average claim involving BI was 510% greater than one that did not.

Further, ransomware incidents at SMEs accounted for 77% of claims with a BI component. The five-year average BI cost for ransomware incidents was $1.4M with a total incident cost of $2.3M. In 2025, these numbers were $510K and $818K, respectively.

## Recovery Expense

### SMEs
294 claims reported recovery expense. As Figure 20 shows, recovery expense has been quite variable, with events in 2022 and 2023 having noticeably higher costs. The total incident cost of these events has dropped since 2023. Even so, the average five-year incident cost of these claims is almost 350% higher than incidents without recovery expense.

Ransomware incidents accounted for 72% of the claims with reported recovery expense. The five-year average incident cost of these events was $1.1M, 400% higher than incidents without recovery expense. In 2025, these incidents cost 250% more.

![Average Recovery Expense SMEs]  
*(Figure 20)*

### Large Companies
Six large company claims reported recovery expense spanning in the period 2021-2025. Recovery expense for these incidents ranged from $19K to $1.0M (average=$427K). The corresponding incident cost ranged from $24K to $15.0M (average $5.7M).

So far, we have collected no claims with recovery expense in 2025. That may change next year as we collect additional data for 2025.

> Recovery cost is where technical debt shows up on the claim file. Modern, well-documented, cloud-native environments come back quickly. Aging systems, undocumented applications and one-of-a-kind equipment don’t—and every additional day of rebuild compounds the interruption loss sitting underneath it.  
> — **Scott German, Principal, RSM US LLP**

## Records Exposed

As we look at the five-year window, we see the number of claims reporting records exposed continuing to decrease. The 2021–2025 range contains 354 claims that reported more than one[^1] record exposed, whereas the 2020–2024 range contained 436 of these claims. These incidents exposed over 720M records—270M at SMEs and 450M at large companies—similar to numbers reported last year.

We cannot pinpoint why the number of claims with exposed records is decreasing, nor can we say whether this represents a change in exposure or a change in reporting. However, we can speculate:
- The large proportion of ransomware and BEC claims since 2020 do not involve exposed records.
- Perhaps (as we have speculated in the past) the lack of utility of per record metrics is causing insurers to be less concerned with the number of records than they once were.

Figures 22 and 23 illustrate the number of exposed records year-by-year and a five-year average. There is no clear pattern. As found in previous NetDiligence Cyber Claims Reports, the number of records exposed does not correlate well with either the size of an organization or the total incident cost.

## Third-Party Incidents

Ransomware (N=7) was a primary cause of loss. The ransoms paid for these claims ranged from $2M to $25M. Total incident costs ranged from $355K to $25M.
