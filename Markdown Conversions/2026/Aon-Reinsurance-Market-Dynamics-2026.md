capital and the macroeconomic environment offer insights on the potential direction of the global

Topic Commentary
Abundant Capacity Capital was abundant with supply significantly outstripping demand. Strong net-
retained earnings have led traditional reinsurers to deploy more capacity while the
presence of third-party capital is more pronounced. More diverse sources of capital
are increasing their participation in the market through a broad range of established
and innovative structures, further expanding the market’s ability to take on risk at
favorable terms. Capacity has also flowed into the market via new players in the form
of start-up reinsurers and managing general agencies, which continue to gain traction
and bring further competitive tension to renewals.
Stable Demand Reinsurance demand was broadly stable, with an average 5 percent year-on-year
increase in limit purchased at the 1/1 renewal. The main exceptions are Italy and
15
Greece where demand has increased by 10 to 20 percent following recent changes
to natural catastrophe insurance schemes. Limit purchased also increased by 10
to 20 percent in Turkey due to inflation and exposure growth, which was partially
offset by movements in foreign exchange rates. Post renewal, insurers are expected
to take advantage of favorable market conditions and premium savings to purchase
additional protection.
Natural Catastrophe Strong competition at the January renewal was supported by a quiet U.S. hurricane
Losses season, with relatively few losses ceded to the reinsurance market in 2025. Global
insured losses from natural catastrophes in 2025 are set to exceed $100 billion for
the sixth year in a row, driven by the California wildfires in January, which caused
insured losses of approximately $40 billion, and U.S. severe convective storms in H1.
However, the remainder of the year was relatively benign with no major hurricanes
making landfall in the U.S. Hurricane Melissa, which struck Jamaica in October as a
Category 5 storm, caused insured losses in the low single-digit billions and had little
influence on the 1/1 renewal.

Topic Commentary
Buying Power Retention levels and attachment points, which were raised significantly in challenging
2023 renewals, were largely unchanged at 1/1 renewals (on a nominal basis, but are
higher from a risk-adjusted perspective). While reinsurers are now willing to negotiate
lower deductibles, most buyers are broadly comfortable with current retention
levels, preferring to focus on pricing and terms. Market conditions have also enabled
insurers to push back on restrictions and restore coverage lost in the hard market,
namely around terrorism, riots and cyber.
Earnings Protection With fierce competition for peak perils and top layers, reinsurers are demonstrating
greater appetite and flexibility around earnings protection and frequency covers.
January renewals saw growing interest among insurers and reinsurers for such
products, including aggregate solutions, reinstatement protection, multiple event
and top-and-drop covers, as well as structured solutions. Reinsurers are also
more actively seeking risks with elevated margins, such as wildfire and severe
convective storm.

and coverage to hedge climate change, sustain the global value chain and access

wildfire exposure through parametric structures when traditional markets fell short.

Topic Commentary
Robust Demand Demand for facultative reinsurance remains robust, with buying patterns largely
reflecting changes in insurance and reinsurance conditions. The simplification of direct
insurance placements – which has seen fewer insurers writing larger lines on large
commercial risks – is driving increased demand as insurers use facultative reinsurance
solutions to write higher limits. Demand is also increasing as insurers use facultative
solutions alongside treaty to support growth and manage volatility as they expand into
new lines of business and geographies.
Capacity Growth Facultative capacity has grown significantly over the past twelve months as competition
with New Players in reinsurance and insurance markets has diverted more capital into the facultative
space through a variety of structures. New players continue to enter the market, while
reinsurers, insurers and managing general agencies have increased their appetite for
facultative reinsurance as competition in their respective markets has increased. Notably,
reinsurers are showing growing interest in supporting facultative facilities, providing
insurers with efficient access to additional capacity through both proprietary and hybrid
facilities. In 2025, Aon’s established commercial risks’ facility Aon Client Treaty was
extended to support global facultative clients.
Hybrid Facilities In addition to single risk facultative solutions and multi-cedent proprietary facilities,
single cedant facultative facilities have emerged as a powerful tool for insurers looking to
grow their portfolios. Often referred to as treaty/fac hybrids, cedant facultative facilities
provide insurers with an efficient, flexible, customized alternative that blends the
efficiency of automatic treaty-style capacity with the precision of facultative placement.
This represents a revolutionary shift in facultative buying from a tactical necessity to a
strategic enabler, targeting specific portfolio segments to meet defined client objectives.

Topic Commentary
Increasing Capacity for casualty reinsurance is broadly increasing, supported by strong appetite
Capacity Supply from current reinsurers and new entrants to the market, and the growing relevance of
third-party capital. Over the past twelve months, third-party capital providers have
supported a diverse range of reinsurance structures in the casualty market, including
sidecars and internal reinsurance vehicles. Such structures provided additional capacity
at favorable terms, building overall competitive tension. The renewal also benefited
indirectly from competition within the property catastrophe reinsurance and short-tail

maintain a holistic whole-client approach, including support for casualty remain the
most relevant.
Strong Demand Reinsurance demand is strong overall and retention levels remain stable. However,
casualty insurers increasingly are recognizing the benefits of rate increases, underwriting
and claims management actions taken over the past two years reflected in improved
results and more adequate reserves, while relatively high interest rates continue to
support attractive investment returns. Many casualty insurers are now experiencing
increased buying power at renewals and are generally more confident when evaluating the
balance of net retained versus ceded risk.
United States Renewals for U.S. casualty reinsurance were broadly positive at January 1. Increased
capacity helped maintain stable conditions, resulting in flat or slightly improved pricing for
most placements. Capacity was ample as many reinsurers looked to grow their lines and
there were no major reinsurers looking to reduce. While the effects of nuclear verdicts,
legal system abuse and third-party litigation funding remain key concerns for the market,
the strong underlying rating environment for U.S. casualty is helping maintain a demand/
supply equilibrium.
