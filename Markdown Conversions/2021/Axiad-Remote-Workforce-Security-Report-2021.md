## Overview

Securing the remote workforce has become a critical priority for organizations affected by the closing of offices and workplaces in the wake of the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic.

This 2021 Remote Workforce Security Report reveals the state of securing the new workforce. The report explores key challenges and unique security threats faced by organizations, technology gaps and preferences, investment priorities, and more.

Key findings include:
- Phishing threats (71%) and malware (61%) have emerged as the most significant new threat vectors concerning remote work environments.
- The top three policies and protocols that employees are most resistant to complying with include multi-factor authentication (35%), mobile device management (33%), and password managers (26%), making it difficult for them to be fully and securely authenticated to all of their applications, devices, etc.
- Seventy-eight percent of organizations agree that it is critical to very important for security tools to adapt to a changing digital environment and that people should not have to configure them.
- Almost half of all organizations (45%) are shifting from multiple, siloed security tools to a platform solution.

## Dramatic Increase in Remote Workforce

In the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic, a majority of organizations made the shift to a remote workforce for almost all employees with 75% of companies having more than 75% of their employees working from home. A year later with the end of the pandemic in sight for some, 60% of organizations still have over 75% of their workforce remote. We expect a good portion of the current remote workforce to permanently stay remote.

## Workforce Expansion Paths

To bolster the capacity for providing employees with secure access to corporate resources, organizations purchased more user licenses for existing applications (47%), more hardware (29%), added additional vendors (26%), and purchased more cloud applications (19%). Thirty-one percent of organization did not expand secure access capacity.

How did you expand your capacity for providing remote employees with secure access to corporate resources?

- We purchased more user licenses for existing apps: 47%
- We have not expanded secure access capacity: 31%
- We purchased more app hardware: 29%
- We added additional vendors/solutions: 26%
- We purchased more cloud apps: 19%
- We replaced existing solutions with new vendors: 12%

## Migration to Cloud

The majority of respondents (54%) are accelerating their move to cloud-based applications. However, despite transitioning to a cloud environment, and the advantages it offers for an integrated security solution, organizations have not transitioned their security controls. Seventy-nine percent of security professionals enforce the same level of security controls and data management for all roles when accessing corporate resources remotely.

Has COVID accelerated migration of additional user workflows or applications to cloud-based applications?  
**YES: 54% | NO: 46%**

Do you enforce the same level of security controls and data management for all roles in the company as they access remotely?  
**YES: 79% | NO: 21%**

## Security Support Issues

Since moving to working remotely, there has been an increase in phishing attempts reported by 66% of security professionals. This is followed by reports of malware on devices (30%) and concerns over connecting personal devices (29%). For 61% of respondents, remote workers have impacted their helpdesk wait times. These increases in attack attempts and wait times are challenging security professionals, taking time and IT resources away from strategic projects that can be costly to an organization. Having to wait longer for help is also having a detrimental effect on productivity of remote workers.

Since going remote, which of the following have you received increased security support questions about?

- 66% reported phishing attempts
- Reports of malware on devices: 30%
- Concerns over connecting personal IoT to same internet network used for work: 29%
- Suspicious two-factor authentication attempts: 28%
- Unable to share/back-up from certain device types: 25%
- Reporting breaches of third-party tools: 24%
- Concerns about terms of service of certain apps: 16%
- Other: 6%

Has remote work impacted your helpdesk wait times?  
**YES: 61% | NO: 39%**

## Shifting Security Burden

Almost half of all organizations (45%) are shifting from multiple, siloed security tools to a platform solution. When asked about the shift in the burden of securing the remote workplace, 80% of professionals somewhat (54%) to fully agree (26%) that it is not the individual employee’s responsibility.

Sixty-one percent of organizations have an identity proofing/verification process before issuing users with identity credentials. Twenty-two percent of professionals don’t have a process, but plan to. As the burden moves to the employees, companies need to prioritize verifying each employees’ identity so they can trust who is on their network.

Do you agree with the following statement: Much of the burden of responsibility for securing the remote workplace is shifting from the IT security team to the individual employee.  
**YES (Fully agree): 26% | Somewhat agree: 54% | NO: 20%**  
*(80% somewhat to fully agree that it is not the individual employee's responsibility)*

Do you currently have an identity proofing/verification process before issuing users with identity credentials?  
**YES: 61% | No, but we plan to: 22% | NO: 17%**

## Adherence to Security Policies and Protocols

The top three policies and protocols that employees are most resistant to complying with, and instead find workarounds over half the time, include Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) (35%), Mobile Device Management (MDM) (33%), and password managers (26%), making it difficult for them to be fully and securely authenticated to all of their applications, devices, etc. This means that even if a company has invested in strong authentication technology like MFA, they are still at risk unless they can encourage employees to comply with their policy. This is even more challenging with a remote or hybrid workforce, as employees are not in the office to work with their IT team to deploy and utilize new technologies. Fifty-two percent of security professionals have experienced employees finding workarounds to corporate security systems and policies.

Which of the following security protocols are individuals most resistant to adhering to or maintain?
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): 35%
- Mobile Device Management (MDM): 33%
- Password managers: 26%
- Other: 6%

Have you experienced employees finding workarounds in your security system/policies?
- File encryption: 24%
- Anti-phishing: 21%
- Virtual Private Network (VPN): 20%
- Anti-virus/malware: 19%
*(Total experiencing workarounds: 52% overall)*

## Barriers to Securing Remote Work

When asked about the biggest impediments to scaling security for remote workforces, organizations named remote work equipment (40%), bandwidth restrictions (39%), and logistics of installing agents on personal devices (27%) as the key barriers. Bandwidth issues emphasize that remote work is significantly impacting productivity. As a result, IT’s bandwidth is more focused on assisting users than on strategic projects.

What have been the biggest impediments to scaling security for your remote workforce?
- Equipment for remote work (devices, cameras, accessories, etc.): 40%
- Bandwidth restrictions impacting productivity: 39%
- Logistics of installing agents on employees’ personal devices: 27%
- We have not experienced security scaling issues: 26%
- Not enough security staff: 26%
- Monetary requirements for buying more or better security appliances: 22%
- Not enough security licenses: 18%
- Not enough security budget: 17%
- The logistical challenge of installing more or better security appliances: 16%
- Increase in helpdesk calls: 1%
- Other: 4%

## Security Tools Adapting to Changing Environments

Due to the limited bandwidth of IT teams, 78% of organizations are looking for adaptable tools that people won’t have to configure. Tools that can be integrated on a platform solution instead of managed in silos are also increasing in popularity, likely for the same reason. As noted by almost half of the respondents (45%), IT teams are looking for ways to simplify the day-to-day maintenance of their various security solutions, which streamlined platform solutions accomplish.

How important is it that security tools can adapt to changing digital environments, without requiring humans to configure them?  
**Critical: 37% | Very important: 41% | Somewhat important: 20% | Not important: 2%**  
*(78% agree that it’s critical to very important for security tools to adapt to the changing digital environment)*

Going forward, do you expect your organization to shift from multiple, siloed security tools, in favor of platform solutions that cover the entire digital infrastructure?  
**YES: 45% | NO: 13% | Not sure: 42%**
