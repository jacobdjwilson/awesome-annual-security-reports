INTRODUCTION
The 2021 Insider Landscape Report reveals the latest trends and challenges facing organizations in
this new environment. The report explores how IT and cybersecurity professionals deal with risky
insiders and how organizations are preparing to better protect their critical data and IT infrastructure.
Key findings include:
• Security professionals are more concerned about inadvertent (71%) or negligent (60%) data leaks
than malicious data breaches (52%)
• 52% of people find it more difficult to detect and prevent insider attacks than external cyber attacks
• 62% of people are concerned or very concerned about insider risk
• Only 29% of organizations currently use an insider risk management solution, despite being more
concerned about accidental data leaks
• 65% of budgets will either stay the same or decline
This 2021 Insider Landscape Report has been produced by Cybersecurity Insiders, the 500,000-
member community for information security professionals to explore how organizations respond to
the evolving security threats in the cloud. We would like to thank Code42 for supporting this unique
research.
We hope you find this report informative and helpful as you continue your efforts in protecting
your IT environments against insider threats.

INTERNAL VS.
EXTERNAL ATTACKS
Data security breaches have become much more difficult to assess. Trying to block all risky activities
can have a huge impact on productivity and innovation. In order to successfully address insider risk,
organizations must shift from policy-based to risk-based cybersecurity approaches. To make this
transition, companies need to consider their insider risk tolerance — the delicate balance between
protecting valuable data and enabling speed, collaboration and innovation.
When comparing internal attacks to external cybersecurity attacks, 52% of respondents confirm that
internal attacks are more difficult to detect and prevent than external cyber attacks. Since insiders
have approved access privileges, it can be challenging to distinguish legitimate use cases from
malicious attacks.

RISKY INSIDERS
Given the diversity of users that pose an insider threat, security teams need the ability to prioritize
the risks that matter most, while also being able to see everything - all data activity, across all
vectors and all users – on and off the network. To accomplish this, they need to see deeper context
so they can parse risky activity and users from legitimate work. They need the ability to orchestrate
highly automated, right-sized responses. Most of all, they need to commit to protecting the business,
without sacrificing speed, collaboration, and innovation among users.
The survey indicates that privileged IT users/admins (55%), contractors (53%), privileged business
users (51%), and regular employees (49%) pose the biggest insider security risk to organizations.

ACCIDENTAL INSIDERS
An important tenet of insider risk is that the majority of data leaks are accidental, not malicious.
Regardless of intention, data leaks jeopardize the financial, reputational, or operational well-being
of a company, as well as its employees, customers, and partners. Based on feedback from security
professionals, there are several ways these accidental breaches occur.
Cybersecurity experts indicate that risky activity like sending business information to personal emails
(62%) and copying information to cloud share (45%) are common causes of accidental insider incidents.
